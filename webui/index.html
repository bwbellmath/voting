What the data covers, and what it does not
  • Results: MIT Election Lab general-election returns, 1976–2020. House is by district, Senate and President by state. When a district is selected for a state-level office, it shows its state's result.
  • Simulations: House and Senate contests were re-run 200 times with 1,000 compass agents each. Presidential contests were not simulated; for President only the random ballot (win chance = vote share) differs from the real result. Seats shown for President are electoral votes, winner-take-all by state (the Maine and Nebraska district splits are ignored).
  • Districts: boundaries are per Congress (Lewis et al. for 1976–2014, Census Bureau for 2016–2020). The 2020 map uses the 116th Congress lines, so North Carolina's 2020 redraw is not shown. A selected district is followed by number across redistricting, so "TX-7" in 1980 and 2020 are different places.
  • Demographics: the repository has no census data below national level. The tab shows what the compass model infers (voter-type mix, party identification) next to Gallup's national survey.
  • State legislative elections: not in the repository, so not shown.
